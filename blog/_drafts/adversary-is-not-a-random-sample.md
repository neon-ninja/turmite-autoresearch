title: "An adversary is not a random sample"

The attraction of active attacks is that they search where a claim is weakest. That same
selection makes a pooled success percentage a poor estimate of how often a rule forms a
highway. A hill-climber deliberately keeps difficult starts; its successive candidates share
most of their cells. Treating them as independent Bernoulli trials manufactures precision.

The falsification harness therefore keeps the counterexample search but withdraws its pooled
confidence interval. A probabilistic claim needs a specified distribution and a separate
sampling protocol. A universal claim can still be challenged by one reproducible witness.
Neither a timeout nor survival of the attack suite proves eventual behavior.

There is a second selection trap in reducing a witness: shrinking can preserve a detector
artefact on one board while destroying its reproduction on another. Every accepted reduction
now has to survive both geometries. This remains an empirical detector check, not a proof of
infinite-time dynamics. See the falsification experiment's appended statistical correction.
