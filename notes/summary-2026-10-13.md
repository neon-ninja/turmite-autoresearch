# Summary of results (2026-10-13)

Compiled from `paper/main.typ`, `paper/numbers.json` and `notes/`. No new measurements; see the source files for commands and seeds.

## Headline
Generalised Langton's ants that don't build a highway grow as **t^(1/3) in width and t^(2/3) in area**. The census covers 3,512 canonical rules of length 3-12.

| Measure | Ants (blob rules) | Rotor walker (known: 1/3, 2/3) |
|---|---|---|
| Width exponent | 0.333 (bootstrap 95% interval 0.332-0.334) | 0.326 |
| R_g exponent | 0.336 | 0.339 |
| Area exponent | 0.679 | 0.687 |

- **Bias correction:** after subtracting the rotor's finite-window bias, the three exponents come out at 0.340, 0.330 and 0.658. They point in different directions, so the claim is consistent with 1/3 at about +/-0.01, not "equal to 1/3".
- **Rule length:** the exponent doesn't depend on rule length (Spearman rho near 0).
- **Classes:** 304 rules build highways. Of the rest, 2,622 are blob growers and 425 grow logarithmically, with a width exponent around 0.10. Of the blob rules, 103 grow faster than a cube root.
- **Noise:** most of the spread between rules is run-to-run noise. Within-rule sd is 0.016 against a between-rule true sd of 0.004.

## Why: the three-factor decomposition
The area growth rate is dA/dt = c * kappa * P/A, where:
- c is the chance an edge step enters new ground.
- kappa is the fraction of time on the edge, relative to a uniform spread over the territory.
- P is the edge length.

This is an identity, so matching it is a consistency check and not a test of the mechanism. The tested content is:
- **c is flat:** the drift exponent is under 0.05 for 99.7% of blob rules.
- **kappa is flat:** the same holds for 83% of blob rules, and for six rules run to 2^32 steps.
- **Edge exponent:** P ~ A^beta with beta ~ 0.52, the same as the rotor in the same window. It is not shown to be exactly 1/2. Local slopes drift toward 0.5, at 0.51-0.53 for 2^26-2^28.
- **Outliers:** rules that deviate from the cube root do so because kappa drifts. The correlation of the width exponent with kappa's drift exponent is 0.78. The log-growers have kappa near 0 and falling.
- **What sets c:** a rule-letter model explains R^2 = 0.66 on held-out rules. The first letters matter most, and R on young colours lowers c. The model mostly separates clusters.
- **Well-mixed ant:** this holds for 35 of 40 random core rules and 24 of 33 convenience-sampled ones. Rules with non-uniform interior colours are the exceptions.

## Frontier work (32 items, all done except the knockout map, which another agent owns)
- **Exact exponent (item 4):** 11 rules x 24 seeds to 2^30. Six match the rotor's finite-time value and five don't (0.660-0.696). The asymptote is undecided, and RRLLLLLLR looks non-self-averaging.
- **Shape (item 5):** the limit shape is round (anisotropy 0.07). Visit density is a cone, 3(1-r/R), for ants and rotor alike to within a few percent.
- **Edge width (item 7):** the ants' boundary-width exponent is 0.25-0.27, against 0.13-0.16 for the rotor. The universality class isn't decided.
- **Other settings:**
  - **Hex and triangular grids (item 24):** area exponents 0.712 and 0.676, against 0.682 on the square grid.
  - **Turmites (item 25):** 85% of 2,290 chaotic growers lie within 0.1 of 2/3, and a separate exact sqrt(t) cluster of 1,560 tables exists.
  - **Many ants (item 27):** the exponent is unchanged.
  - **Higher dimensions (item 28):** extent 0.245 and volume 0.788, consistent with 1/4 and 3/4.
  - **Noise (item 26):** the cube root survives noise up to eps <= 0.1.
- **Highways (items 17-20):**
  - **Unresolved rules:** of 807 rules run to 10^10, 38 (4.7%) were new highways. The pre-registered ~1% was falsified.
  - **Catalogue:** 96% are diagonal and the period is always even. Period is independent of n, which falsified a prediction, and period 18 is the most common at 22%.
  - **Predictors:** predicting chaos versus highway from the rule is weak (AUC 0.70).
- **Literature (item 29):** Bou-Rabee and Peres prove a convex limit shape, not a disc, and there is no proof route for reversible ants.

## Tethered ("hard") rules
These are the log-growing rules that stay near home.
- **Pattern-matched fields:** no local statistics up to 3x3 rebuild a home. What holds the ant tracks exact cells. Keeping a random 90% of cells in place holds the ant better than a field matched on 3x3 statistics. A degraded field gives a graded outcome, not an on/off one.
- **Knockout map (two rules):**
  - **Hot spots:** 3.5% of 5x5 tiles are reproducible hot spots, against 0.7% expected under the null.
  - **Size of the effect:** the worst 5x5 window removes 20-30% of the tether, and even a 3x3 window can cost 15-18%.
  - **Where:** the sensitive region is a disc of radius about 15-20 around the tether centre.
  - **Return routes:** the return-route idea (P3) is not supported once occupancy is controlled. Partial rho is 0.13 and 0.03, and both intervals contain 0.
  - **Contiguous patches (P4):** a contiguous patch does no worse than the same number of scattered cells, which was unpredicted.
- **Other findings:**
  - **Critical cells:** rare colours are critical in every tethered rule tested.
  - **Home types:** 556 tethered rules share only about 28 discrete home types.
  - **Graded hardness:** hardness is graded and bimodal in the size-free measure.
  - **Corruption threshold:** the critical corrupted fraction doesn't depend on age.

## Unexplained (open questions)
- Why kappa takes its value, and why it drifts in some rules.
- Whether the edge exponent is exactly 1/2.
- Why ant visit density equals the rotor's cone.
- Why the ants' edge is wider than the rotor's.
- Why there are only about 28 home types.
- Why rare colours are the critical cells.
- Whether RRLLLLLLR is truly non-self-averaging.

## Caveats stated in the paper
- Exponents come from 7-octave windows.
- Each rule has one trajectory.
- Rule length is capped at 12.
- 153 rules weren't fitted, and the fastest growers are probably under-represented.
- Highway classification is finite-time.
- The paper's data-availability, AI-disclosure and funding sections are still placeholders.
